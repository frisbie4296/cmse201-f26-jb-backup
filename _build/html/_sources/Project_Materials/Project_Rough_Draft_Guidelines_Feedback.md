# Rough Draft Guidelines/Feedback

## Why are we doing this?
Aside from just checking in to see your progress, we also want you to give feedback to your groupmates on their project notebooks. Critiquing the work of others is an incredibly valuable skill that we want you to practice. Because Jupyter notebooks allow for a synergy between writing words and code, there are different criteria for evaluation. 

## Two things to keep in mind for this rough draft
1. We don't expect you to have completed the project. We expect you to have part of your methodology and some results completed. (Although your full procedure should be clear to the reader, even if it hasn't been implemented yet.)
1. The following categories are criteria that will be used to assess the final draft of your semester project. The author of the notebook can't really ignore any of these things. 


## Criteria for evaluation
For each of these criterion, take notes on any changes you think might help the notebook author improve their project. Bring your notes to class. 

#### *Does the Notebook Run?*
The submitted notebook (along with auxiliary materials) should run. In Jupyter Notebook, go to `Cells` and select `Run all`.     

#### *Clear research question* 
Is the research question clearly stated at the beginning? Is it easy to find?

#### *Clear Methodology*
You should unpack/explain your steps along the way. Your audience are your fellow CMSE 201 students, meaning that any tool and/or package must be thoroughly explained, both in terms of what it does and what it produces. 

#### *Informative Plots*
Use the Day 20 ICA rubric to evaluate the effectiveness of the plots being presented. 

#### *Compact Code*
There shouldn't be any large chunks of repeated code. If the same procedure is used multiple times, it should be either turned into a function or a for loop. 

#### *Informative and Unique Variable Names*
The variables used in the code should indicate their purpose (I.e., you shouldn't see a lot of `a' or `x` variables). There also shouldn't be any repeated variable names.
