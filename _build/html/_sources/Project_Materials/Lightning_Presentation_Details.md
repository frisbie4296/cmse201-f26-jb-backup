# Lightning Presentation Details

## Summary
- **Give a short (<90 seconds, <=2 slides) presentation on your project**
  - *Goal is to get people interested in your work*
  - *Presentation should focus on (preliminary) results*
- **Give feedback to classmates on presentations**

## Why are we doing this?
For these presentations, you will be trying to sell your project to the audience–i.e., you want to capture your classmate’s attention. Selling your work is something that all professionals have to do, so it’s important to get practice now. It is also important to practice giving constructive feedback on other presentations. Plus, it’s good practice for your final presentations at the end of the semester.

## What will you need to do?
Submit one to two slides (two max) for your presentation. You will have 90 seconds (maximum) to talk through your slides, so it is **strongly encouraged** that you practice at least once to get the timing right. 
Your presentation must include the following elements:
- **(1 Point) Your research question** 
- **(1 Point) A brief description of your methodology (no more than two sentences)**
- **(3 Point) Some preliminary results presented as a plot/figure that tells part of the story of your work.** 

These are lightning presentations, so they will be light on details. The emphasis should be on showing something interesting/unexpected that will get people interested in your work!

On the day of the presentations, you will be responsible for giving your presentation and providing feedback to (some of) your classmates.

## How will we do this?
Your instructor will share either a Google Sides or OneDrive Powerpoint presentation with the entire class, where you can upload your slide(s). The slides will be sorted alphabetically, though the actual order of the presentations may vary. **Make sure to upload your slide(s) early so you can handle any formatting issues that may come up.**

For larger sections, the timing will be tight. The section lead will keep track of time and may need to cut presentations short if they go too long. Again, we **strongly encourage** you to practice your presentation beforehand to get the timing right.
